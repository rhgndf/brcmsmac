# BCM43227 support in brcmsmac

Target: PCI `14e4:4358` (subsystem `105b:e040`), BCM43227 rev 0 package 8,
D11 core rev 30, N-PHY rev 16 (LCNXN, normalised +16), analog rev 9,
radio 2057 rev 9 version 1, 2.4 GHz only, two chains, SPROM rev 8,
`extpa_gain=2` (internal PA on 2.4 GHz).

Constraints:

- brcmsmac is ISC. b43 is GPL-2.0-or-later. b43 code and findings may be
  used; each commit that does names what it took from b43 and where.
- Values for this chip come from the reference MMIO capture of wl
  6.30.223.271 (firmware 832.127) on this card
  (`../b43/.work/captures/wl-init-ht20-ch6-02/` and the later wl captures
  next to it), from existing brcmsmac, or from b43.
- No proprietary source, disassembly or decompilation. wl is observed only
  as a black box: MMIO, DMA and over-the-air captures. Firmware is taken
  unmodified, by symbol name, from `wlc_hybrid.o_shipped` of the
  broadcom-wl 6.30.223.271 package (`../broadcom-wl-dkms`, installed at
  `/usr/lib/broadcom-wl-dkms/wlc_hybrid.o_shipped`).
- The port series (up to `800781c`) was verified without loading the
  module: build (W=1), checkpatch, and scripted re-derivation of every
  data table. Every later commit was tested on the card in the lab
  described below.
- Each commit builds, keeps existing devices unchanged, and is reviewable
  alone. The device is bound only by `800781c`.
- Pre-existing brcmsmac bugs are not fixed here; they are listed in
  [EXISTING_BUGS.md](../EXISTING_BUGS.md). A fix that a new feature
  cannot work without stays in that feature's commit and says so.

Why brcmsmac rather than b43: [bcm43227-feasibility.md](bcm43227-feasibility.md).

## Firmware

wl 6.30.223.271 ships, in brcmsmac's native layout (LE32 ucode words;
`struct d11init` LE16 addr/size + LE32 value, `0xffff` terminated):

| Symbol | Size | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `d11ucode30_mimo` / `d11ucode30_mimosz` | 40640 B | core 30 N-PHY ucode |
| `d11n16initvals30` | 3784 B | core init values |
| `d11n16bsinitvals30` | 288 B | band init values |
| `d11ucode_ge24_bommajor/minor` | 832 / 127 | version |

The capture used this same firmware, so its SHM/descriptor ABI is the
reference for the MAC side.

  above the other rates and wl. `328102e`

## Lab testing

The BCM43227 is a station in the root network namespace (192.0.2.2). The
peer is an RTL8812AU (rtw88) in network namespace `b43-peer` running
hostapd as an access point (192.0.2.1): `b43-lab-43227`, channel 6, HT20,
WPA2-PSK CCMP, `[SHORT-GI-20]`, beacon interval 100 TU, DTIM period 2.
For 40 MHz, `.work/hostapd-ht40.conf` runs HT40- on channel 6 and
`.work/tmp/hostapd-ht40p-ch1.conf` HT40+ on channel 1. The station's
wpa_supplicant configuration pins 2437 MHz, so channel 1 needs
`WPA_CONF=.work/tmp/wpa-anyfreq.conf`. For 20/40 MHz coexistence the
peer runs `.work/rtw-coex` (the lab rtw88 advertising
`NL80211_FEATURE_AP_MODE_CHAN_WIDTH_CHANGE`) and a lab hostapd
(`.work/hostap/hostapd-2.12`) on `.work/hostapd-ht40-coex4.conf`, and
`.work/tmp/coex-loop.sh` injects a 40 MHz intolerant coexistence frame
into hostapd each cycle. A home TP-Link access point
(HT20) with a wired iperf3 server served as a second peer.
Throughput is iperf3 TCP over 8 s unless noted, TX meaning from the
BCM43227 to the access point. Numbers vary by ±10 Mbit/s from run to run,
so each comparison alternates builds over several rounds.

AP mode needs no change for this chip. With the BCM43227 running hostapd
(same settings) and the RTL8812AU as its client, WPA2 association, HT20
MCS15 short GI both ways, 71.8 Mbit/s to the client and 45.1 Mbit/s from
it. With the client in power save, unicast frames wait for the TIM and
arrive (28-210 ms pings), but broadcast frames are lost: brcmsmac sends
them before the DTIM beacon (EXISTING_BUGS.md).

Known lab behaviour that no commit addresses:

- Bidirectional TX starvation. With TCP in both directions at once, the
  BCM43227 transmits only ~1.5-3 Mbit/s against 20-80 Mbit/s received.
  Hypothesis, not verified: BE A-MPDUs are capped near 250 per second,
  and fq byte fairness lets the TCP ACKs of the download crowd out the
  upload's data.

Findings at 40 MHz:

- The hardware sends at most 63 null delimiters. With 64 or more in the
  TX header, every attempt ends in a TX PHY error. 100-byte UDP at
  MCS 13-15 short GI needs more for 16 µs spacing, so `0cd5ae0` caps the
  count. wl writes larger counts (87 and 105 at MCS 15), mostly uses
  MCS 13 with 60, and sends 100-byte UDP at 2.6 Mbit/s.
- Not adopted: after each 40 MHz channel set, wl also sets
  `IFS_CTL1_EDCRS_20L` and `IFS_CTL1_EDCRS_40` (0x003f in IFS_CTL1 and
  SHM `M_IFS_CTL1`, against 0x000f at 20 MHz). Writing them at run time
  had no measurable effect.
- Not adopted: HF4 bit 0x0020, which wl sets. With it, brcmsmac's
  traffic breaks.
- Left as is after a 40 MHz association: PHY 0x69, 0x222, 0x234, 0x323,
  0x324 and IHR 0x656/0x658 differ from wl, as do calibration results
  (PHY 0x9a-0x9d, 0x29c, radio 0x172). Writing wl's values into IHR
  0x656/0x658 and PHY 0x324/0x29c at run time did not change the
  TX PHY error rate.
- 20/40 BSS coexistence: the station follows the AP from 40 to 20 MHz
  and back without reassociating, and TCP keeps running (20-120 Mbit/s
  per 5 s). Since the AP sends OBSS scan parameters, wpa_supplicant
  scans every 10 s or so, about 2.8 s per scan under traffic; a report
  naming a 20 MHz neighbour moves the AP to 20 MHz by itself. wl as the
  station follows the same switches (chanspec 0x1904 and 0x1006), but
  its TCP download stopped for 2-30 s after most of them.
- Lab hostapd: rtw88 does not advertise
  `NL80211_FEATURE_AP_MODE_CHAN_WIDTH_CHANGE`, without which hostapd
  ignores coexistence reports. With `no_pri_sec_switch=1` hostapd skips
  its OBSS scan, keeps no secondary channel and never returns to 40 MHz;
  the lab copy sets it from the configuration
  (`src/ap/hw_features.c`).
- RX FIFO overflows came with the AP's width changes: up to 461 per
  30 s in downloads with them before `411cc39`, 1-5 per 30 s after.
  Plain 40 MHz downloads, scans and OBSS scans had none with either
  build. wl keeps up to 64 receive buffers posted (MMIO trace, refilled
  8 at a time), brcmsmac 32 (`NRXBUFPOST`). With 64 the overflow counts
  did not change and TCP down was 122.9 against 119.8 Mbit/s over ten
  rounds, within run-to-run variation, so it is not adopted.
- HT capabilities in the association request: wl sends HT capability
  information 0x187e and A-MPDU parameters 0x17 (4 us spacing since
  `d11379e`, as brcmsmac now). Its information lacks TX STBC (0x0080),
  which brcmsmac sets since `704d6e9`. At 40 MHz it adds MCS 32 to its
  receive MCS set; brcmsmac does not.

Monitor mode and weak signals:

- A mac80211 monitor interface works without changes: the MAC runs
  with `MCTL_PROMISC`, `MCTL_BCNS_PROMISC` and `MCTL_KEEPCONTROL`. In
  6 s, all 58 probe frames the microcode counted arrived, 6 of 8
  control frames and 54 of 71 beacons; why the rest did not is open.
- The BCM43227 decodes weak signals poorly with either driver. A
  neighbouring AP beacons at 1 Mbit/s on channel 6 under three BSSIDs,
  which the RTL8812AU receives at -76 to -80 dBm: 176-186 beacons per
  BSSID in 20 s. In monitor mode, unassociated, brcmsmac and wl
  (`WLC_SET_MONITOR`) each decoded 1-8 per 10 s at 20 MHz. At HT40-
  brcmsmac decoded 6 of 1626 with the `40373ea` settings and 3 of 1654
  without, while every lab AP beacon arrived. Too few to judge
  `40373ea` (`.work/tmp/moncmp.sh`). With the stock rtw88, the
  RTL8812AU's monitor sees nothing while a station is associated with
  its AP; with the lab copy in `.work/rtw-coex` it does.

## Open questions

Answered by the wl trace: the core 30 FIFO partition (9/98/22/14/14/5,
trace 11530-11564); SHM writes (same offsets and values, item 11); the
N16 -> radio rev 8 channel table reuse (correct apart from TXMIX2G/PAD2G,
item 5).

Per-rate output power, read as signal at the AP from a monitor
interface on it (`.work/tmp/pwrcap.sh`), differs between rates as the
targets brcmsmac computes from SPROM rev 8 and its locale do
(`.work/patches/txpwr-dump.patch`).
The SPROM limits are 19 dBm for CCK, 17 dBm for OFDM and 20 MHz MCS, and
16 dBm for 40 MHz MCS. The 2.4 GHz limits cap CCK at 17 dBm, OFDM and
SISO at 16 dBm, and the two-chain modes (CDD, STBC and, since `328102e`,
SDM) at 13 dBm. With 1.5 dB taken off, the per-core targets are
15.5 dBm for CCK, 14.5 dBm for OFDM and SISO, and 11.5 dBm for the
two-chain modes. At 20 MHz, wl's MCS 0-15 arrive at the level of
brcmsmac's after `328102e`; at 40 MHz its MCS 12-15 arrive 2 dB lower.

The secondary A-MPDU TX status was never late in the lab. brcmsmac
gives up on it after about 10 us; the wl trace reads it right after the
primary word. A lab build that logs every wait
(`.work/patches/s2-trace.patch`) found it valid on the first read
throughout 75 s of uploads at HT40: TCP, bidirectional TCP, and UDP with
100- and 1400-byte datagrams.

The 832.127 A-MPDU TX descriptors captured at HT40
(`.work/captures/wl-txdesc-ht40-ch6-01`), which gave the bandwidth
fields and the delimiter count, differ from brcmsmac's
(`brcms-txdesc-ht40-ch6-01`, `.work/txhcmp.py`) in three ways.
PreloadSize, MaxNMpdus, MaxABytes and MinMBytes are 0 in both:

- wl sets `TXC_IGNOREPMQ` (0x0020) in MacTxControlLow on every A-MPDU
  member (0x032f/0x0523/0x0723 against 0x030f/0x0503/0x0703). Without
  it the microcode suppresses frames to receivers with a PMQ entry
  (`TX_STATUS_SUPR_PMQ`), clients in power save, which a station does
  not have. brcmsmac sets the bit only on beacons.
- wl falls back from MCS 15 to MCS 12; minstrel gives brcmsmac MCS 7.
- wl's Duration and FragDurFallback are 48 us (SIFS + BlockAck), and
  brcmsmac's 44 (SIFS + ACK), on air at HT20 too. See
  `EXISTING_BUGS.md`.

Answered in the lab: A-MPDUs are received and Block Ack sessions work in
both directions (TCP under rate control: RX 54-78 Mbit/s, TX 60-75
Mbit/s). Outside an A-MPDU the microcode delivers an A-MSDU as one DMA
frame per subframe (`100090e`). The joined frame takes RxTSFTime from
the last piece, and that is the start of the A-MSDU, like a whole
frame's. The first piece's RxTSFTime is stale: in 1232 of 3336 A-MSDUs
at MCS7 it equalled the previous frame's. Taking each frame's stamp plus
its airtime, the next frame's stamp came at least 90 us later (SIFS, ACK
and AIFS) between whole frames and with last-piece stamps, apart from 3
jumps of about 20 ms in each. With first-piece stamps, 726 of 3605 gaps
around an A-MSDU were shorter than 80 us, down to -340 us. b43's G13
assumed the reverse.

The A-MSDU loss at legacy rates (`100090e`) comes from the lab peer. At
fixed OFDM rates from 24 to 54 Mbit/s with 600-byte datagrams, A-MSDUs
of N whole subframes (44 + 652N bytes) arrived. Others came 184 bytes
shorter (1164, 1816, 2468, 3120 or 3772 bytes) and ended inside their
last subframe. The microcode acknowledged them (as many ACKs as unicast
data frames, and none of their sequence numbers came again) but did not
pass on the last piece, so the join dropped them: 6-16% of the
datagrams in saturated runs. b43 on the same card lost 8-18% at
54 Mbit/s, 10% at 24 Mbit/s and none at MCS7. At HT rates every A-MSDU
had one of the 44 + 652N lengths (`.work/tmp/amsdu-legacy.sh`,
`.work/patches/amsdu-loss-trace.patch`).

Still open:

- HF4/HF5 bits wl sets (item 11): HF4 0x0020 breaks brcmsmac's traffic;
  the others' effect is unknown.
- A-MSDUs inside A-MPDUs stay declined (no `SUPPORTS_AMSDU_IN_AMPDU`),
  as wl declines them. Since `0dc749e` the receive path takes them. With
  a lab build that accepts them, the home AP sent about 1100 small
  A-MSDUs (TCP ACKs) per 10 s upload, and TCP was unchanged (up
  27.1-29.1 against 24.6-27.1 Mbit/s, down 52.1-55.0 against 49.4-55.3).
  With 3098-byte A-MSDUs from the lab rtw88 (`amsdu_2g`, `amsdu_max`
  4000), ten downloads ran at 46-64 Mbit/s, as without them. After the
  A/B script (`.work/tmp/amsdu-ampdu-ab.sh`), though, every download
  collapsed to 1.3-1.5 Mbit/s with 100-200 retransmits, in some runs
  even after the station was reloaded. The station dropped nothing
  (`skb:kfree_skb`, mac80211 counters). The peer sent one or two MPDUs
  per PPDU, about every 3 ms, its A-MPDU time limit
  (`REG_AMPDU_MAX_TIME` 0x70). The trigger was not found, and the lab has
  no third radio to watch the peer's transmissions.

## Out of scope for this series

- 5 GHz (the chip is 2.4 GHz only). 40 MHz on 2.4 GHz for other chips:
  `0cd5ae0` enables it only on core rev 30.
- Calibration refinements beyond what wl's init sequence shows.
